# CING Manifesto Outline

**Working title:** *Standing Up for Cornwall*
**Status:** Draft outline for group discussion
**Date:** 2026-03-30

---

## Structure

### 1. Who We Are

A short, plain-spoken introduction. Not a political party. No whip. No central office. Three councillors who answer to their communities and nobody else. Explain what nonaligned means in practice — we vote on the merits of every issue, not on what a party leader tells us to think.

End with: this manifesto is what we stand for. It's not imposed from above. It's built from what our communities tell us matters.

### 2. What Cornwall Needs

A brief, honest assessment of where Cornwall is today. Set the scene for everything that follows.

Key points to cover:
- Wages well below the national average, house prices driven up by outside money
- Young people leaving because they can't afford to stay
- Public services stretched — GPs, dentists, social care
- Infrastructure that hasn't kept pace with population growth
- A distinctive identity and culture that deserves to be heard, not managed from Westminster

Tone: factual, not angry. Acknowledge what's good about Cornwall too — community, landscape, resilience — but be honest about the problems.

### 3. Our Principles

Five or six short, clear statements that everything else flows from. These are the non-negotiables that unite the group. Suggested starting points:

1. **Cornwall first** — Every decision judged by what's best for Cornwall, not what a party whip demands
2. **Straight talking** — Say what we mean. No spin, no jargon, no ducking the hard questions
3. **Local knowledge** — The people who live here understand Cornwall's problems better than any national party headquarters
4. **Accountability** — Public money, public scrutiny. No decisions behind closed doors
5. **Fairness** — Cornwall deserves its fair share of national funding and its own voice in how that money is spent
6. **Community** — Strong communities are built by the people in them, not by politicians imposing top-down plans

## Local Policy Chapters (Cornwall Council)

Each chapter should follow a consistent format:
- **The problem** (2-3 sentences, plain language)
- **What we'll do** (specific, concrete commitments)
- **How we'll pay for it / make it happen** (where possible)

### 4. Housing

- Cornwall's housing crisis: local people priced out, second homes and holiday lets reducing supply
- Strengthen local connection requirements in planning
- Regulate the short-term lettings market effectively
- Council must be an active housing enabler — work with housing associations and community land trusts
- New developments must come with infrastructure (schools, surgeries, roads)
- High-quality, appropriately scaled — not sprawling estates dumped on communities without consultation

### 5. Cost of Living

- Low wages and high costs — Cornwall's defining economic squeeze
- Council tax: press for a fairer funding settlement from central government
- Use council procurement to support local suppliers and keep money circulating locally
- Fuel poverty and energy costs in rural areas — support community energy schemes
- Lobby for fair wage policies and support for key worker retention

### 6. Jobs and the Economy

- Cornwall's economy is diverse — small businesses, tourism, agriculture, fishing, creative and tech sectors
- Stop applying one-size-fits-all policies designed for cities
- Invest in the infrastructure businesses need: broadband, roads, town centres
- Stronger partnerships between the Council, colleges, and local employers
- Create pathways so young people can build careers here without leaving Cornwall
- Support sustainable economic growth that benefits Cornish people, not just visitors

### 7. Health and Social Care

- Aging population, stretched services, recruitment crisis for GPs and dentists
- Adult social care is the council's biggest spend — demand adequate government funding
- Mental health services: reduce waiting times, invest in early intervention
- Support rural access — not everyone can travel to Truro or Plymouth for treatment
- Work with NHS partners to address Cornwall-specific recruitment challenges

### 8. Environment

- Cornwall's coastline, moorland, rivers, and hedgerows are assets and responsibilities
- Robust protection of designated sites and habitats
- Council must lead by example on emissions and waste
- Planning decisions must properly weigh environmental impact
- Work with farmers and land managers — they're stewards of the landscape, not the enemy
- Any green transition must come with practical fully costed support, not just targets

### 9. Transport

- Reliable, affordable transport in a large rural county — not just tourist routes
- Protect and improve bus services, including community transport
- Road maintenance: fix the backlog, invest in prevention
- Active travel infrastructure (cycling, walking) should complement the road network, not replace it
- Campaign for better rail services and fair rail pricing

### 10. Planning and Development

- Planning decisions must serve communities, not developers
- Strengthen neighbourhood plans and give communities a genuine voice
- Enforce planning conditions — too many developers ignore what they agreed to
- Review permitted development rights, particularly around second homes and holiday lets conversions
- The Local Plan must reflect Cornwall's needs, not housing targets set in Whitehall

### 11. Education and Skills

- Post-16 provision must give young people real choices
- Strengthen links between further education and local employers
- Address the brain drain — make Cornwall a place where graduates want to stay
- SEND provision: reduce waiting times for assessments and improve support in schools
- Early years: accessible, affordable childcare across the county

### 12. Culture, Heritage and Cornish Identity

- Cornwall has a distinctive culture and identity — it's not just a brand for tourism
- Support the Cornish language, mining heritage, and local festivals
- Culture isn't a luxury — it's part of what makes communities strong
- Protect and promote Cornwall's unique status

### 13. Governance and Transparency

- Cornwall Council spends hundreds of millions of pounds — residents have a right to see how
- Stronger scrutiny committees with genuine power
- Council meetings accessible in person and online
- Seriously consider the committee system over cabinet-style government
- Publish agendas and reports in good time — no governance by ambush

### 14. Community Safety

- Work with police and partners on anti-social behaviour
- Support drug and alcohol services
- Rural crime: take theft, fly-tipping, and agricultural crime seriously
- Ensure community safety partnerships reflect local priorities, not just national targets

### 15. Council Finances

- Press for a fair funding deal from central government — Cornwall is shortchanged
- Transparent budgeting with proper scrutiny
- Prudent management of reserves and commercial investments
- Council tax increases must be justified and proportionate

## National Policy Positions (Parliamentary)

These sections are for the parliamentary candidates. Shorter, principle-based, framed as "Cornwall's voice at Westminster." Not detailed policy blueprints — CING isn't a national party and shouldn't pretend to be.

### 16. A Fair Deal for Cornwall

- Cornwall consistently receives less per head than comparable areas
- A CING MP would fight for a fair funding formula that recognises rurality, deprivation, and distance
- Sustainable economic growth and improved job opportunities for Cornish people

### 17. Immigration

- The system is broken and unfair — to the people trying to use it and to the communities affected
- CING doesn't have a party line on numbers — we want a system that actually works and enforces existing immigration law at our national borders
- Local impact matters: housing pressure, public service demand, seasonal labour needs for agriculture and hospitality
- Pragmatic reform, not performative and divisive outrage

### 18. Cost of Living (National)

- Low-wage areas like Cornwall are hit hardest by rising costs
- Energy policy must work for rural homes, not just urban flats
- Fair wages, affordable housing, accessible public services — the basics that national parties keep promising and failing to deliver

### 19. Health (National)

- NHS recruitment in Cornwall is a crisis — GP deserts and dental deserts
- A CING MP would push for targeted incentives to attract health professionals to underserved areas
- Mental health parity with physical health — in funding, not just rhetoric

### 20. Devolution

- Cornwall deserves the right to decide its own future governance
- Devolution is a fundamental constitutional change — it must go to the people of Cornwall in a referendum
- All pros and cons explained honestly, then let Cornwall decide
- CING will campaign for a fair and informed debate, not push a predetermined outcome

### 21. Fishing, Farming and Food Security

- Post-Brexit promises to fishing and farming communities have not been kept
- Cornwall's primary industries need practical support, fair trade deals, and less red tape
- Food security is national security — domestic producers deserve better

## Closing

### 22. Our Commitment

A short, direct closing statement. Restate the core offer: genuinely local, genuinely independent, genuinely accountable. No party machine. No career politicians. Just people who know Cornwall, live in Cornwall, and will fight for Cornwall.

Include a call to action: stand with us, stand as a candidate, or just hold us to what we've said here.

## Notes for Group Discussion

- **Tone throughout:** Plain-spoken, no jargon, positive but honest. We don't attack other parties by name — we let our independence speak for itself.
- **Evidence:** Where possible, back up claims with local data (wages, house prices, funding per head, service waiting times). This builds credibility.
- **Commitments:** Be specific where we can, honest where we can't. "We will push for X" is better than vague aspirations. But don't promise what a small group of councillors can't deliver.
- **Parliamentary sections:** These are deliberately lighter. A CING MP is Cornwall's voice at Westminster, not a shadow government. Keep the focus tight.
- **Length:** The final document should be readable in 15-20 minutes. Voters won't read a 50-page document. Consider a short summary version (2 pages) alongside the full manifesto.
- **Design:** When finalised, this should be published on the website and available as a downloadable PDF, designed to match the CING brand (Kernow Horizon design system).
